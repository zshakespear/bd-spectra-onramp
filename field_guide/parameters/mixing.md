# Vertical mixing (K<sub>zz</sub>) and disequilibrium chemistry

[← Parameters](README.md) · [notebook 5 §6](../../notebooks/05_global_parameters.ipynb)

**What it is.** The atmosphere is not still: convection and turbulence carry gas up and down (**vertical mixing**).
K<sub>zz</sub> (cm<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, usually quoted as log K<sub>zz</sub>) says how fast. Deep down it is hot and carbon is
mostly CO; high up it is cold and carbon "wants" to be CH<sub>4</sub>. If mixing lifts gas faster than CO can convert,
CO survives into the layers we see: **disequilibrium chemistry**.

**What it changes.** In cold atmospheres, equilibrium chemistry would turn CO into CH<sub>4</sub>; strong mixing brings
CO (and CO<sub>2</sub>) up from below, so the 4.15–4.3 µm and 4.4–4.9 µm notches are deeper than equilibrium predicts,
and CH<sub>4</sub> absorption changes. Sonora Flame Skimmer and Exo-REM include it; Diamondback (equilibrium) does not.

**Where to look.** The 4–5 µm region and the CO<sub>2</sub> and CO [indices](../indices.md).

**What can mimic it.** Metallicity (more carbon and oxygen), C/O ratio, temperature, an overlapping absorber in
the index windows.
