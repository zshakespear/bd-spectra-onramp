# Effective temperature (T<sub>eff</sub>)

[← Parameters](README.md) · [notebook 5 §2](../../notebooks/05_global_parameters.ipynb), [notebook 1](../../notebooks/01_blackbodies.ipynb)

**What it is.** The temperature of a blackbody that emits the same total power per unit area.

**What it changes.** Everything — it is the *global* parameter:
- the overall slope (Planck): short wavelengths change most, because they sit on the steep side of the Planck curve;
- the chemistry and temperature profile, so many bands change together: CH<sub>4</sub> appears after L4,
  CO/CO<sub>2</sub> collapse between T6 and T7 (~1000→700 K);
- where the flux is: below ~580 K the Planck peak is beyond 5 µm; Y dwarfs are detected reliably only beyond ~3.9 µm.

**Where to look.** Ratio to a template: a smooth tilt across the whole range plus changes in *many* bands.

**What can mimic it.** Gravity (in some bands), metallicity, clouds (reddening), an unresolved cooler companion.
A difference confined to *one* absorber's bands is probably not T<sub>eff</sub>.
