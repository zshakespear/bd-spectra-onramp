# Surface gravity (log g)

[← Parameters](README.md) · [notebook 5 §3](../../notebooks/05_global_parameters.ipynb)

**What it is.** log<sub>10</sub> of the surface gravity in cm s<sup>−2</sup>. Brown dwarfs are mostly ~5; young, low-mass
objects have lower gravity.

**What it changes.** Higher gravity → higher pressure at the photosphere → stronger pressure-dependent
absorption, especially H<sub>2</sub> collision-induced absorption (broad, ~1.7–3 µm).

**Observed signs of low gravity** (Rustamkulov et al. 2026, low-gravity sequence at L0 and L5):
- a shallower 0.75–1.2 µm slope;
- weaker water features;
- a taller K peak relative to H.

**Hard to fit.** Gravity's effects are modest and other parameters can produce similar ones — it is often
**degenerate** with them (the data cannot tell the changes apart). Expect a fitted log g to be uncertain.

**What can mimic it.** Temperature (cooler vs higher gravity can look alike in some bands), metallicity
(also acts through H<sub>2</sub> CIA), clouds (a red, taller K peak).
